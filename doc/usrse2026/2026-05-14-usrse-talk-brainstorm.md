this repo is an experiment in using LLMs in-the-loop of a data science task to explore modeling improvements to our powertrain model "routee-powertrain". in our group, we do not have a data scientist, and have when financially feasible farmed out model development to interns. model improvements have been stagnant for a number of years as our team focused on software engineering and transportation engineering problems instead. this experiment begins with our existing work, where we use random forests trained on vehicle trajectory data and FASTSim energy estimates. this approach is decently accurate at the trip-level but varies greatly at the link-level of travel. given we use this in-the-loop of a route planner (routee-compass), link-level accuracy is important. in this experiment, we explored setting up an autoresearch LLM task to explore the feature and ML model type spaces over a fixed powertrain model to discover ways to improve our estimates. we ran an agent that would iterate on models and features and improve on itself over subsequent iterations as a sort of loose metaheuristic search that was also capable of making structural changes to the experimental design in order to open the search parameters beyond our understanding of the field. in the process, the LLM found a few new ideas we were not aware of. it identified the value of adding features for the 5 previous links as well as the sinuosity of the road geometry. it also explored sequential DL models, finding its best results when using a CNN architecture, clearly as it can capture the sequential nature of the link space observations. as a result, we have seen matching trip accuracy but greatly improved link-level model accuracy. in future work, we will explore additional powertrain models to see if ICEV/HEV/BEV powertrains might need different treatments and also analysis of the trends over these revised models, as well as deployment of a next-generation set of routee-powertrain ML models.
